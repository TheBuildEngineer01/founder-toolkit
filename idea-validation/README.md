# Don't build an app. Not yet.

**4 cheaper ways to test your idea before you pay for app development, and how to know when it's time to build.**

Most app ideas don't fail because of bad code. They fail because nobody needed them in the first place. Each test below costs a fraction of an app and answers one question.

---

## The 4 tests

| # | Test | What you do | What it tells you |
|---|------|-------------|-------------------|
| 1 | **A simple landing page** | One page that explains the idea, with a sign-up or "join the waitlist" form. Share it where your customers are. | Do people actually want this? |
| 2 | **Run it by hand first** | Use WhatsApp, a form and a spreadsheet to serve your first customers manually. | Which steps are worth automating? |
| 3 | **A clickable prototype** | A design people can tap through (no real code). Watch real users try it. | Is the flow clear to real users? |
| 4 | **A web app before a store app** | One web app that works on every phone and laptop. Add App Store / Play Store apps later. | Do users need it on their phone every day? |

---

## Scorecard: is it time to build?

Tick what's true for your idea:

- [ ] People signed up or asked to be notified without being pushed
- [ ] You've served real customers by hand and know the exact steps
- [ ] Testers could finish the main task in the prototype without help
- [ ] Customers are asking "is there an app?" or "can I do this myself?"
- [ ] You know which single workflow the first version must do

**4–5 ticks:** good signal. Plan the build.
**2–3 ticks:** run one more test first.
**0–1 ticks:** start with test 1.

> This is a general guide, not a guarantee. Every idea and market is different.
